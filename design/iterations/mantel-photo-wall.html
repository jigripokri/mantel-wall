W3b-1 geometry
Attachment
The clock and the weather sit in one flex row, align-items: flex-end, 40px gap. The weather column adds 40px left padding and a 1px hsl(30 12% 28%) left border — the same hairline weight as the shelf.
Optical seat
6px bottom padding on the weather column so its last line seats on the clock's baseline rather than its descender box.
Temperature
Newsreader 300 / 54px, ink, tabular-nums. Half the clock — clearly the second number.
Condition
Instrument Sans 600 / 26px, .24em, uppercase, ink-dim, 12px above — the same setting as every other label in the system, and on the 26px floor.
Range
Instrument Sans 400 / 26px, ink-faint, tabular-nums, 12px below. Slash form 81° / 58° — shorter than H/L and needs no legend.
Date
Drops the appended weather and returns to Newsreader italic 300 / 32px alone, 14px under the row.
Day skin
Same geometry; ink becomes hsl(28 30% 18%), dim hsl(30 14% 38%), faint hsl(30 14% 52%), hairline hsl(28 22% 74%).

x 96 → 583 at the widest (measured), leaving the whole photo field and the ledger untouched.

Recommendation: auto-generate, admin can overwrite. Asking a parent to write a caption for every upload is a chore they'll do twice and then stop, and a wall of blank mats is worse than no caption at all. The phone already knows everything a good caption needs — the photo's date, who's in the household, and what was on the calendar that day.

The rule, in order: ① if the photo's date matches a calendar event within ±1 day, use the event — “Anvi's 5th birthday”. ② else if it has a place, use place + season — “Alum Rock, last July”. ③ else use warm relative time — “Two summers ago”. ④ if the photo is more than a year old and matches today's date, lead with “One year ago today” — the line most worth reading. ⑤ if none of that resolves, no caption; the mat stays paper.

Constraints: one line, ≤ 42 characters, sentence case, no year numerals (“last July”, not “July 2025”), never a filename or a hashtag. In the app the caption appears as pre-filled text under the photo the moment it's added, so overriding is a tap, not a task.

How many, and which

Three is the number. One is lonely on a 75" wall and makes every rotation an event; six turns the room's centrepiece into a contact sheet you stop seeing. At three, each photograph is still big enough to read a face from the couch (the hero runs ~366×445 of actual image, roughly 14" wide on the panel), and the group reads as a deliberate hang rather than a feed.

Recommendation: P3 hung triptych for the day skin, P6 mantelpiece for the evening. They're the same idea at two temperatures — P3 hangs, P6 leans — so the wall keeps its "one artwork, two moods" rule. P2 stays as the quiet fallback when the family has fewer than three photos queued; P4 is worth keeping for a "this month" review the app can trigger deliberately, not as the resting state.

P5's cropped shoulders read as UI — a carousel implies controls that don't exist on this wall — so it's out.

Frame
Evening mat
Print sits on hsl(40 26% 92%) with an uneven mat — 16–20px on three sides, 52–66px at the foot (the classic instant-print proportion), scaled with the frame. Shadow 0 14px 44px hsl(24 30% 4% / .55).
Day mat
Cream would vanish into oat, so the frame keeps the same mat but gains a 1px hsl(34 24% 82%) rule and a much lighter shadow at 13–17% — the print reads as paper on paper.
Rotation
Never more than ±2.2°, never zero for every frame in a group — two tilted one way, one the other. Rotation is fixed per slot, not per photo, so a new photo doesn't make the wall jump.
Sizes
P3 fills a 956px field exactly: hero 400×520, supports 260×326, two 18px gaps (400+260+260+36 = 956) — frames are flex:none so nothing shrinks. P6 shelf: 400×490 / 300×370 / 270×330 / 210×262. Portrait crops throughout — landscape photos letterbox inside the same frame rather than changing it.
Caption
Only the hero is captioned, Newsreader italic 26px in the mat's foot. Captions come from the phone; with none, the foot stays empty — it's mat, not a missing field.
Rotation over time
Cadence
One frame changes every 4 minutes, never two at once, never the hero twice in a row — so the wall is always mostly still. A full three-frame turnover takes ~12 minutes.
Transition
1.2s crossfade, no slide, no zoom. The frame, mat, shadow and rotation stay put; only the image inside dissolves. Ken Burns is dropped entirely in framed mode — drift inside a printed border looks broken.
Selection
Shuffle within the family's queued set, weighted toward the ten most recently added, never repeating a photo while it's already on screen in another frame.
Fewer than three
2 photos → P2 stack (the peeking pair reads as intentional). 1 photo → P1 single, enlarged. 0 → the layout collapses to the calendar-led states in the main spec; no empty frames, ever.
Night
After 23:00 the mat dims to hsl(40 20% 78%) and shadows deepen 15% — the prints recede but stay legible, matching how the ledger already softens.
